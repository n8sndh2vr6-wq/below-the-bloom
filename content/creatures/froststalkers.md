Fast, armoured predators that hold the [icy trenches](#/locations/icy-trenches)
and everything approaching them. They are the first thing the ocean sends at
Oyster, and the first thing he is completely wrong about.

## Anatomy

Heavy jaws, raking claws, and fins edged with ice-hardened cartilage. That last
detail matters: a fin torn free of a corpse is jagged enough to be swung as a
blade, which is exactly what Oyster does with one in
[Scene 6](#/story/chapter-1/scene-06) when his knife is not enough.

They fight in low, watery rumbles and they bleed into the water like black ink.

## Classes

**Berserker class.** Hunts alone. The one that nearly splits Oyster in half in
[Scene 4](#/story/chapter-1/scene-04) is a berserker, and killing it is the first
thing he does that surprises even him.

**Common class.** Hunts in coordinated packs — dozens moving in formation
through the black, slicing past with terrifying precision. In
[Scene 5](#/story/chapter-1/scene-05) a whole pack passes Oyster and Chip
without stopping, because they are hunting something else.

> **OYSTER:** Something's got them scared.

## The correction

Inside their own territory the Froststalkers are not a pack at all. They are a
civilisation: nests built into icy alcoves, salvage crews hauling pieces of the
crashed ship across frozen bridges, cubs being nudged along by adults growling
encouragement, and patrols moving in visible order.

> **OYSTER:** Maybe they're not monsters after all.
>
> **CHIP:** Or maybe everyone's just a monster in someone else's story.

They keep [prisoners](#/story/chapter-1/scene-09) rather than eating them, and
their [king](#/characters/froststalker-king) releases those prisoners when they
protect his young. By the end of the chapter they are the only kingdom left
standing.
