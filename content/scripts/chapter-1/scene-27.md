Oyster and Chip glide downward, following the glowing purple trail that circles a fish darting through the current. Strangely, the darkness begins to fade—natural light shimmers from below. It’s the first time they don’t need their lights.

OYSTER: Well, I’ll be. Something down here’s actually… pleasant.

CHIP: Mark the calendar. First sunlit moment in days. I was starting to think the ocean had a personal vendetta against photons.

OYSTER (Smiling): We should thank our little guide. Leading us right to dinner.

Oyster jets forward, slicing cleanly through one of the fish with his knife.

CHIP: Finally. Protein. I was two thoughts away from biting my own circuitry.

But then—

The water stills. The glowing fish begins to shimmer, crackle—its body contorting. Limbs extend. Plates form. Scales harden. It stretches and grows at a terrifying rate, morphing into a massive, ancient sea beast—like a fusion of a shark and a dragon, its body the size of the iceberg that sank the Titanic.

CHIP: Oh no. Oh no no no—OYSTER YOU JUST STABBED A BABY EMERGENT.

OYSTER: THAT WASN’T MARKED ON THE SCANNER!

The Emergent lets out a deep, resonating groan, then opens its colossal mouth. Water surges, pulling Oyster and Chip toward it like a black hole.

OYSTER (gritting his teeth): Not today—!

He pulls out his oxygen pressure blaster, turns it downward, and FIRES. The pressure shoots them back against the surge, barely escaping the vortex of its maw. They tumble behind a rock outcropping, panting in the silence.

CHIP (Dryly): Congratulations. You’ve managed to stab what might be the oldest being on this planet.

OYSTER (Heavy breathing): I thought it was a fish! I WAS HUNGRY!

CHIP: You’re always hungry. One of these days it’s going to kill you. Possibly today.

OYSTER (Glancing out from cover): Well, if that thing’s the ruler of wherever we are now… we’re officially trespassing in the gods’ garden.

CHIP: Next time maybe don’t shank the garden gnome.

They stay hidden, the Emergent moving slowly through the open water beyond, casting shadows the size of buildings.
