The interior glows with dim blue lights. A large window shows the icy planet below—rolling clouds, shimmering auroras, and a surface of endless, shifting water. Instrument panels hum. OYSTER (16), lanky, in his pressure suit (unzipped at the collar), lounges in a chair. CHIP, his AI companion, floats in a dormant state nearby, projecting soft diagnostic lights. Two researchers, DR. LENA VOSS (30s, calm, assertive) and TECH OFFICER MARIQ LEVI (20s, jokester, optimistic), work at the front controls.

MARIQ (laughing): Alright, I’m just saying—if this planet’s covered in ocean, there better be alien sushi down there. Or I’m filing a complaint.

OYSTER (smirking): You wouldn’t even make it through the first scan. You’re scared of shrimp.

MARIQ: I’m not scared of shrimp. I just—look, anything with too many legs and no eyebrows? That’s suspicious.

DR. VOSS (cutting in gently): Focus, boys. We’re minutes from descent. Let’s make sure everything’s stable.

CHIP (booting up with a flicker): Stability confirmed. External pressure systems normal. Internal crew patience… wearing thin.

OYSTER (mocking): You trying out for stand-up again, Chip? You’re not funny. You’re a glorified calculator with sass.

CHIP: I was designed to match my operator’s attitude. You set the bar low.

Everyone laughs. Even Voss smiles slightly.

DR. VOSS: Honestly, this is good. I want this energy. After the last three expeditions? We deserve one that goes right.

MARIQ: Yeah, what’s the worst that could happen—mutant krakens? Evil algae?

Oyster gazes out the window, something soft behind his eyes.

OYSTER (low): We’re not here just to explore. We’re here because this planet doesn’t fit the rules. The readings… they don’t make sense.

CHIP: Confirmed. Planetary core anomalies. Heat signature inconsistencies. Subterranean structures—unknown origin.

DR. VOSS: That’s why we’re here. If there’s intelligence down there… it changes everything. Maybe answers about the ones who came before us.

MARIQ (suddenly quieter): Still gives me the creeps. Like something’s… watching us already.

The lights flicker for half a second. The hum of the ship shifts tone. Everyone freezes for just a beat.

OYSTER (tensely): Did anyone else see that?

CHIP: Power flux. Minor. Likely caused by proximity to planetary magnetic field.

Then—

DR. VOSS: Wait—what is that?

On the monitor: a pulse of purple light expanding from the planet’s surface, like a ripple through space itself. It begins to glow brighter—impossibly bright.

CHIP: Warning. Electromagnetic surge inbound. Impact in five… four…

The crew scrambles. The ship lurches. Systems spark and flicker violently.

MARIQ: What is that?! What’s causing it?!

CHIP: …Two… one.

The purple wave hits the vessel. The lights explode into white. Sirens blare. The ship spins violently, alarms shrieking.

DR. VOSS: We’re losing altitude! I’m initiating the crash protocols—Oyster, Mariq, brace!

MARIQ: I can’t stabilize it—it’s dragging us down—!

Through the front window, they see it: the glowing violet ocean growing larger and larger beneath them. A storm churns. The ship is screaming downward.

OYSTER (terrified, barely above the noise): We’re going in—!

CHIP: Brace for impact—

The screen cuts to black as the vessel plunges into the depths.
